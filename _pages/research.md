### Neutrino oscillations and the mass ordering

Neutrinos come in three types, and they change from one type to another as they travel. This effect, called [neutrino oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_oscillation), shows that neutrinos have mass, which the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model) of particle physics does not predict. I work on what oscillation experiments can tell us about which neutrino is the heaviest (aka the mass ordering), whether neutrinos and antineutrinos behave differently (aka [CP violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation)), and how to squeeze every inch of physics out of our experiments.

Key papers: [arXiv:2611.13629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13629), [arXiv:2007.08526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08526), [arXiv:1904.02751](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02751)

### Short-baseline anomalies and sterile neutrinos

For more than two decades, a few experiments ([LSND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_Scintillator_Neutrino_Detector), [MiniBooNE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiniBooNE), and others) have seen more events than expected. One proposed explanation is a fourth, ["sterile" neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterile_neutrino). I have combined data from many experiments to test this idea, and proposed alternative explanations and novel experimental signatures of them. I have proposed several ways to expand the physics program of experiments designed to address this question, such as [SBND](https://sbn-nd.fnal.gov/), [MicroBooNE](https://microboone.fnal.gov/) and [ICARUS](https://icarus.fnal.gov/).

Key papers: [arXiv:2503.13594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13594), [arXiv:2111.10359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10359), [arXiv:1807.09877](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09877), [arXiv:1303.3011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3011)

### New physics and dark matter at neutrino experiments

Large neutrino detectors are also very sensitive general-purpose instruments. I study how they can search for [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter), new forces and new particles, and how precision measurements such as the [weak mixing angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weinberg_angle) can be made with them.

Key papers: [arXiv:1912.06658](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06658), [arXiv:1907.08311](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08311), [arXiv:1903.10505](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10505)

### Simulation of neutrino interactions

Interpreting neutrino data requires accurate simulations of how neutrinos interact with atomic nuclei. I co-develop [Achilles](https://github.com/AchillesGen/Achilles), a Monte Carlo [event generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_generator) for electron- and neutrino-nucleus scattering, and study how modeling uncertainties affect measurements.

Key papers: [arXiv:2502.19467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19467), [arXiv:2210.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03753), [arXiv:2205.06378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06378)

### Machine learning and AI for physics

I use machine learning to improve reconstruction and classification of neutrino events, and I work on AI-driven workflows for scientific software development.

With collaborators, I designed an [agentic lab notebook](https://zenodo.org/records/23072254) in which AI agents translated the code of a high-energy physics application. The design and the full record of agent runs are public.

Key papers: [arXiv:2610.09995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09995), [arXiv:2511.11938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11938), [arXiv:2405.15867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15867)
