I am a theoretical physicist at Fermilab. I study neutrinos, the lightest known matter particles. They are produced in huge numbers by the Sun, by nuclear reactors and by particle accelerators, but they interact so rarely that detecting them takes large, sensitive experiments. Their properties are among the few places where we have clear evidence that our current theory of particles is incomplete.

My work connects theory with experiments. I develop models of new physics, compute what experiments such as [DUNE](https://www.dunescience.org/), [SBND](https://sbn-nd.fnal.gov/) and [MicroBooNE](https://microboone.fnal.gov/) should observe, and build the statistical, simulation and machine learning tools needed to compare predictions with data. I am a member of the DUNE and SBND collaborations.

**What this involves in practice**

- Leading research projects and teams: Principal Investigator of a DOE SciDAC project (USD 3.25M, 2025) and recipient of a DOE Early Career Award (USD 2.5M, 2019).
- Statistical analysis of large datasets, global fits, and sensitivity studies.
- Scientific software: Monte Carlo event generators such as [Achilles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06378), machine learning for event reconstruction, and AI-assisted software workflows.
- Coordination and service: co-coordinator of Fermilab's Neutrino Physics Center, chair of the Neutrino Theory Network steering committee, member of the DUNE Program Committee.
- Teaching and communication: lectures at international summer schools and talks for general audiences.
